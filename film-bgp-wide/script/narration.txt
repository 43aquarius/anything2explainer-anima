# BGP — The Internet's Diplomats — narration v1 (| splits subtitles only; EN block <=48 chars)
# CHAPTER 1 The Vanishing
October fourth, twenty twenty-one.|The internet forgets Facebook.
Instagram, WhatsApp — gone.|Everywhere at once.
Not a hack. Not a cable cut.|The address itself... vanished.
The internet has no map.|No one holds the master list.
Every network announces where things are —|and every other network believes it.

# CHAPTER 2 A Network of Networks
The internet isn't one network.|It's about a hundred thousand of them:
ISPs, clouds, universities —|each one an autonomous system, an AS.
Each has a number, and speaks|for its own corner of the map — a diplomat.
These diplomats trade notes|with a protocol called BGP:
"I can reach this address block,|through these networks." Pass it on.
Your packet hops from AS to AS —|a chain of strangers,|each trusting the last.
Stitch every announcement together,|and the map draws itself.
No boss. No central brain.|Just a hundred thousand voices agreeing.

# CHAPTER 3 Trust Has No Locks
But a spoken map can lie.
Nineteen ninety-seven:|one small provider announced|more than it owned —
and the internet believed it.|Large parts of the net went dark.
Two thousand eight:|Pakistan blocked YouTube at home.
The block leaked —|and the world's YouTube traffic|went with it.
BGP trusts the more specific claim:|name the exact address, and you win.
No signature. No password.|Just a voice in the dark.
## gap 15

That October day, Facebook withdrew|its own routes, by mistake, from inside.
Within minutes, the map stopped|knowing Facebook's name.
For almost six hours,|billions of people hit reload.

# CHAPTER 4 Signing the Map
The fix is arriving:|cryptography for the map itself.
With RPKI, the real owner of an address|signs: "these routes are mine."
A false announcement can be checked —|and simply ignored.
Trust, replaced by proof.
And the map still redraws itself,|every few seconds,
a hundred thousand diplomats|saying where everything is.
It works because they keep talking.
The internet has no map.|It remembers one — out loud.
