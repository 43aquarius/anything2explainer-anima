# WiFi — The Conversation in the Air — narration v1 (| splits subtitles only; EN block <=48 chars)
# CHAPTER 1 The Shared Air
You can't see your WiFi.|You can't hear it.
But the air in this room|is full of conversations.
Radios can't talk and listen|at the same time.
Your laptop, your phone, your TV —|they take turns on the same air.
One talker at a time.|That is the whole rule.

# CHAPTER 2 Taking Turns
So how does everyone|not talk over each other?
Before speaking,|your device listens first.
If the air is busy,|it stays quiet and waits.
When it finally talks,|it waits a random extra beat —
just in case someone else|had the same idea.
That little pause|is why your network works.
No referee. No schedule.|Just good manners,|built into the radio itself.
## gap 20

# CHAPTER 3 The Crowd
Move into an apartment,|and the air gets crowded.
In the two-point-four gigahertz band,|only three channels don't overlap:|one, six, and eleven.
You and your neighbors share them,|like it or not.
And there's a trickier trap:
two devices that can't hear each other,|but both can reach the router.
Both talk at once —|and neither is heard.
So the router lets them|announce themselves first:
a small request, a small reply —|then the floor is theirs.

# CHAPTER 4 The Promise
This all began in nineteen ninety-seven —|two megabits, if the air was kind.
Today, the same air carries movies.
Faster radios. Wider bands.|The engineering never stops.
But when your video stalls for a breath,|then plays —
that's not the network failing.|That's the air, taking turns.
One sky.
One voice at a time.
