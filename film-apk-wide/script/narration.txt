# CHAPTER 2 The Box
1989.|Phil Katz, PKWARE, Milwaukee.
He had just been sued over his archiver, PKARC,|by the makers of ARC,|and his answer to the lawsuit|was a new format: ZIP.

His initials are still in the file.
Open any zip in a hex editor:|the first two bytes are P, K.
Fifty, 4B.|Every APK on your phone starts with them.
## gap 15

A zip is not really about compression, though.|It is an index.
Local file headers,|a central directory,|and at the very end,|a record that says where everything is.

That index is why Android picked it.
The system can open one file inside the package|without unpacking the rest.
The tooling was everywhere, too,|twenty years of it by 2008.
And Android was built on Java:|a Java app already shipped as a JAR,|which is a zip with a manifest inside.
## gap 15

One more twist.
Not everything in an APK is compressed.
The resource table must be stored uncompressed,|aligned to four-byte boundaries,|so Android can map it straight into memory.
The box is smarter than it looks.

# CHAPTER 3 The Seal
So you tap Install.
The phone is holding a zip from the internet,|a hundred and twenty megabytes from a stranger.
First question:|has anyone tampered with it?
## gap 15

The old scheme, v1, was JAR signing|from the Java world:|a signed receipt for every file inside the box.
To check it, Android had to walk|every entry and hash it, one at a time.

Android 7.0 added scheme v2,|and it works like a wax seal|around the whole package.
The signing block sits between the files|and the central directory,|and the signature covers the entire APK.
Change one byte anywhere,|and the install dies.
And installs got faster:|no more walking every file.
## gap 15

The key is the developer's identity.|There is no certificate authority.
You sign your own app,|and only the same key can ship an update.
## gap 12

Verification passes.|Now Android reads the label.
The manifest is binary XML:|name, version, every permission the app wants,|every screen it can show.
The package gets copied to its home,|under /data/app.
## gap 12

Then the real magic: dex2oat.
Older Android ran apps through an interpreter,|Dalvik, a few lines at a time.
Since Android 5.0, the runtime is ART,|and at install it compiles classes.dex|into machine code for your exact CPU.
That is the optimizing bar you see|when your phone updates itself:|every app on the device, recompiled.
A single dex file tops out|at 65,536 methods.|Big apps just carry more of them.
## gap 15

Finally, a sandbox:|/data/data, one Linux user per app.
The icon appears.|Ten seconds, done.
## gap 15

And here is the thing:|the app never got unzipped.
The zip is still sitting there in /data/app,|being read in pieces.
