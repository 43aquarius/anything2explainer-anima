Rename any APK to .zip and it just opens. Every Android app ships as a ZIP archive — a format from 1989. Google picked it in 2008 for a brand-new phone operating system, and it never changed. The format's inventor, Phil Katz, had just been sued over his first archiver; the ZIP file he built to answer it starts with his initials, P and K — still the first two bytes of every app you install.

And installing is not unzipping. In the ten seconds after you tap Install, Android checks a v2 signature that covers the entire file, reads the binary manifest, compiles classes.dex into machine code for your exact CPU with dex2oat, and gives the app its own sandboxed Linux user. The file is never unzipped — it sits in /data/app and is read in pieces.
