# TLS — The Little Lock — narration v1 (| splits subtitles only; EN block <=48 chars)
# CHAPTER 1 The Open Postcard
It's a coffee shop.|You connect to the free Wi-Fi and log in.
The person at the next table|is running a packet capture.
They can see every site you visit,|every request you send —|in plain text.
HTTP is a postcard.|Anyone on the path can read it as it passes by.
In 2010, a Firefox extension|called Firesheep made this a one-click game —|log in as someone else,|sitting right next to them.
## gap 30

The problem was old.
In 1994, Netscape needed people|to trust a credit-card number|to an open network.
Their answer was SSL —|a lock on the envelope.
It grew up, survived its mistakes,|and became TLS.
Today's version, TLS 1.3,|was published in 2018.

# CHAPTER 2 The Handshake
Under the lock, the first move|is a handshake that takes a single round trip.
Your browser sends a ClientHello —|its list of ciphers and a key-share.
The server answers with its own hello|and its certificate.
Then something strange happens:|both sides compute the same secret —|without either one sending it.
## gap 25

From here on, everything is|symmetric encryption, AES-GCM.|Fast, stream-like, sealed.
The person at the next table|still sees every packet.
The handshake itself is public.|The envelopes still fly,|addressed and timed.
But the letters inside|have become noise.
## gap 20

# CHAPTER 3 The Magic Trick
How do two strangers agree on a secret|on a channel everyone can read?
This is the Diffie-Hellman trick,|published in 1976 —|fifty years ago.
Each side mixes a private color|into a public one.
Anyone watching sees the mixes —|and cannot un-mix them.
The math version|relies on one-way doors:|multiplying is easy,|undoing it is not.
## gap 15

That gives TLS a property|worth saying out loud:|forward secrecy.
If the server's private key|is stolen tomorrow,|the recordings from yesterday|still cannot be decrypted.
The past stays sealed.
TLS 1.3 makes this mandatory.|No exceptions, no weak suites —|five cipher suites, down from thirty-one.
## gap 15

# CHAPTER 4 The Chain of Trust
One question is left:|who vouches that this server|really is shop.example?
A certificate —|a stamp from a Certificate Authority.
Your browser ships with|about a hundred and twenty|of these authorities built in.|Trust, pre-installed.
A man-in-the-middle attack fails here:|the fake certificate isn't stamped|by anyone your browser believes.
In 2015, Let's Encrypt|made certificates free and automatic.|HTTPS stopped being a luxury.
## gap 15

Today, more than four out of five|page loads travel over TLS.|The little lock is just... normal.
And the lock keeps being re-forged:|encrypted ClientHello is now standard,|and post-quantum key exchange|already protects most browser traffic.

So — the coffee shop.
The person at the next table|captured thousands of envelopes.
They can see who you talked to,|when, and how much.
But every letter they open|is noise.
Everyone can see the envelope.|No one can read the letter.
