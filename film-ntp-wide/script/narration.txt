# CHAPTER 1 The Drift
Your clock is wrong.|Right now.
Not broken — just drifting.|A quartz crystal counts the seconds,
and quartz drifts|a few of them every single day.
For a wristwatch, that's fine.|For the internet, it's a problem.
Login codes expire in thirty seconds.
Certificates are valid|only between two exact moments.
A database told the same thing|writes it down in a different order.
On a network,|wrong time isn't a nuisance.|It's a contradiction.

# CHAPTER 2 The Pyramid
So who has the right time?
Atomic clocks do —|the world's best,|run by national labs.
GPS satellites each carry one,|and whisper the time down to earth.
But your laptop can't afford|an atomic clock.
So it asks a server —|and that server asks another,
each one a little further|from the source.
NTP calls each layer a stratum:
stratum one talks to the atom;|stratum two asks stratum one —|and so on, down to fifteen.

# CHAPTER 3 The Cross-Check
But networks lie a little.|Every answer arrives late.
So your laptop asks|four servers at once.
Their answers disagree —|of course they do.
The trick: trust the ones|that agree with each other.
The odd one out gets dropped.|The crowd keeps the time.
And here's the gentle part:|no sudden jumps.
The clock just speeds up,|or slows down a little,
until it walks quietly back|into step.

# CHAPTER 4 The Committee
Who runs all this?|Volunteers.
A public pool, thousands of servers,|lending the internet their time.
Since nineteen eighty-five, NTP|has kept the world's computers agreeing —
across borders, across vendors,|across twenty-seven leap seconds.
It isn't one perfect clock|ruling them all.
It's thousands of ordinary ones,|checking each other — forever.
Time isn't kept.
It's agreed.
